# U.Psy — Compliance and security audit, 5 November 2026

Scope: the production platform (upsy.ma, Supabase project in London, Cloudflare
Worker, edge functions) audited against the frameworks an investor's due
diligence, a hospital partner or a regulator will use. This is a readiness
audit, not a certification: ISO 27001 and SOC 2 need an accredited external
auditor, and only the CNDP can authorise processing under Law 09-08.

**Verdict: technically ready for an external audit; administratively not yet.**
The code-level controls are in place and tested (27 database test files run on
every pull request). What remains is paperwork and settings only the founder
can do: the CNDP filing, multi-factor sign-in for staff, and written policies.

## Before entering another African market

Each law needs local registration and, for health data, usually local consent
and transfer rules: Nigeria (NDPC registration, NDPA 2023), Kenya (ODPC
registration, DPA 2019, health data classed sensitive), South Africa
(Information Regulator, POPIA section 26–32 on health information), Senegal
(CDP), Côte d'Ivoire (ARTCI). Specialists must hold a licence in the client's
country: cross-border sessions are the main legal risk of an Africa-wide offer.

## What institutional investors will check next

Security is necessary, not sufficient. A Series A investor in African health
tech checks: completed sessions per month and their growth, specialist
retention, revenue per session after payouts, cost to acquire a client, and
clinical outcomes measured with validated scales (PHQ-9, GAD-7 already built
in). The go-to-market plan sets the 90-day targets for these.
